Independent, self-directed studyApril 2026

Florida grocery market-entry study

Full report, 12 pages (PDF) Deck, 14 slides (PDF)

Seven Florida markets ranked on eight weighted criteria, with an interactive capital model and a Tampa Bay recommendation.

Where should a Northeast-based regional grocer open its first Florida store, and what would it take? I ranked seven markets on eight weighted criteria, screened sites, and built a capital framework that separates land, store, distribution, and launch costs. The recommendation: enter through an inland Tampa Bay growth corridor.

  • 7 markets compared
  • 8 weighted criteria
  • $227M to $327M initial program

The decision

A promising customer profile was only part of the question. I weighed income and growth against competition, land, labor, operating risk, and distribution access. The Tampa Bay corridor led because it scored well across several of those constraints and could sit inside a central Florida distribution network.

What I produced

Market comparison, site screening, a capital framework, a written report, and a board-format presentation. There was no client: I scoped, researched, modeled, and wrote it on my own, using a Northeast-based regional grocer's Florida entry as the scenario. Retailer and exact site identifiers are left out.

Next steps before an investment decision

Full-stack software2026

Agentic OS

A scheduling and monitoring dashboard for automated jobs, with live output, retries, and a full run history.

A scheduling and monitoring dashboard for my own automated jobs. It shows what ran, what failed, and what needs another attempt, with live output, retries, and success-rate tracking, on self-hosted TrueNAS and Docker infrastructure. Every run records an outcome, and future work is routed by measured success rate rather than fixed rules.

Stack: Next.js · React · SQLite run records · TrueNAS · Docker

One decision that mattered

Every run keeps its own record, so a retry never erases the first failure. When something breaks I can see when, why, and whether the second attempt fixed it, the same audit trail an operations team expects from any process.

What it covers

Cron scheduling, streamed output, concurrency limits, and per-run retries, on infrastructure I administer myself: TrueNAS, Docker, private networking, and backup and sync jobs.
